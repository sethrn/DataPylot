Scaling your numeric features is an essential part of many machine 
learning workflows. Some algorithms, especially those that rely on 
distance calculations (like k-nearest neighbors or support vector machines), 
are sensitive to the range and distribution of feature values. Features 
with larger numeric ranges can dominate the behavior of a model unless 
all features are brought onto a similar scale.

In this section, you can apply one of several common transformation 
techniques to one or more numeric features in your dataset:

Standardization transforms the data into a distribution with a mean of 
0 and a standard deviation of 1, also known as z-score normalization. 
It is often a safe default and works well when your data is roughly 
normally distributed.

Log transformation is useful for compressing the scale of highly skewed 
features, often those with a long tail or exponential growth patterns. 
It can help reduce the impact of extreme outliers and stabilize variance. 

Square root transformation is a lighter alternative to log transformation 
and can be helpful for moderately skewed data, especially when your values 
are non-negative.

You can apply any of these techniques to multiple features at once. 
Scaling doesn’t change the relationships between features, but it can make 
those relationships easier for a model to understand when it has to account 
for all of the features at once.
