Many Machine learning models typically require numerical input. If your 
dataset contains categorical values, such as names, labels, or grouped 
categories, you might need to convert them into a format that models can
interpret. This process is called feature encoding, and in DataPylot, 
you have several encoding options to choose from:

Ordinal encoding assigns a unique integer to each category 
based on order of appearance. This method is simple but assumes an 
inherent order to your categories, which may not always be appropriate.

One-hot encoding creates a new column for each category and marks its 
presence with a binary indicator (1 or 0). It's well-suited for unordered 
categorical data and is commonly used in models that can handle many features.

Binary encoding takes a more compact approach, converting categories into 
binary numbers spread across fewer columns. This is useful when working with
features with high-cardinality (many different possible values).

For full control, custom encoding allows you to manually assign numeric values 
to each category. This is helpful when you know the relative importance or 
relationship between categories that standard encoders can't capture. 
You can also use this to apply a custom Ordinal encoding.

You can apply encoding to any feature, whether it's currently categorical 
or numeric. If your goal is to group continuous numeric values into discrete 
bins, such as labeling age ranges or income brackets, you may prefer the 
Bin Features section instead.
