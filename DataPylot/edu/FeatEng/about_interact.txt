Creating interaction terms is a powerful technique for capturing 
relationships between features that aren't captured by looking at them 
individually.
In DataPylot, this operation allows you to multiply two numeric features 
together to produce a new feature, one that represents their joint effect.

This is especially useful when you suspect that the influence of one variable 
on your target depends on the value of another. For example, in a housing 
dataset, multiplying Square Footage by Number of Bedrooms might yield a more 
informative feature than either on its own. Many machine learning models, 
especially linear ones, can benefit from having these interaction terms 
explicitly included.

When creating interaction terms, it's important to consider how correlated 
the original features are. If two features are highly correlated, their 
interaction term may not add much new information and could introduce 
redundancy or multicollinearity. In general, it's a good idea to retain 
the original features alongside their interaction terms during feature 
engineering, then assess their combined value during model training or 
feature selection. This lets the model decide which signals are most 
predictive.

To create higher-order polynomial terms (like squaring a feature or raising 
it to a power), simply repeat the operation using the same feature multiple 
times. This lets you capture more complex, nonlinear patterns in your data 
without manual transformations outside of the tool.

By generating interaction terms, you're expanding the feature space in a 
way that can boost model accuracy, as long as the interactions are meaningful 
and not overly redundant.
