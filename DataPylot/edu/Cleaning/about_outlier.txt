Outliers are data points that deviate significantly from most of the other 
data points feature. While not always errors, they can distort statistical 
summaries and negatively impact the performance of machine learning models.
There are two strategies for handling outliers, Winsorization and removing
rows/instances.

Winsorization modifies the dataset by 'capping' outlier values past a defined 
boundary, replacing their value with this capped value. This is useful when
you want to keep the value, but not allow it to skew the behavior of a model.
You can apply this cap to lower bounds, upper bounds, or both.
With DataPylot, you can only apply Winsorization to numeric values.

Removing outlier rows is the other option, and this is helpful when outliers
represent noise or errors, or there are enough data points left over.
In DataPylot, you can apply outlier removal feature-by-feature or to the
entire dataset (where any row with an outlier is dropped)

Regardless of how you choose to handle them, you’ll first need to detect 
outliers. DataPylot provides multiple detection methods, also available in
the Detect Outliers window in the EDA section:

Tukey’s Fence: Uses the interquartile range (IQR) to define acceptable bounds. 
This is used for skewed or non-normal distributions.
Z-score: Flags values that are a set number of standard deviations away from 
the mean. this is better suited for normally distributed data.

Custom Threshold: Set your own upper and/or lower bounds manually for full 
control.
